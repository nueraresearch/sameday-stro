# Market Benchmark and Sweet-Spot Analysis

This benchmark compares the current Sameday STRO pricing and payout model against external market references for:
- cleaner pay,
- host-facing cleaning fees,
- mileage,
- affordability vs profitability tradeoffs.

## Sources used
1. Indeed: Colorado house cleaner salary — average **$23.12/hour**, low **$14.81**, high **$36.34**, based on 910 salaries updated Oct. 4, 2026.
2. Colorado DLSS: 2026 statewide minimum wage **$15.16/hour**.
3. IRS: 2026 business mileage rate **$0.725/mile**.
4. AirDNA: average U.S. Airbnb cleaning fee in 2025 **$161.10/stay**; example U.S. 2-bedroom average around **$141.60**; professional cleaners often **$25–$60/hour** according to AirDNA’s guide text.
5. Homeyou (Alamosa): single-family house cleaning baseline **$100–$130** in Alamosa; page last updated May 11, 2026.

## 3. Mileage benchmark
### Your mileage model
- Host charged: **$0.60/one-way mile**
- Cleaner paid: **$0.35/one-way mile**
- Gross retained spread: **$0.25/mile** before overhead/risk

### External market signal
- IRS 2026 business mileage rate: **$0.725/mile**

### Interpretation
Your cleaner mileage pay of **$0.35/mile** is materially below the IRS business mileage benchmark.
That does **not automatically mean it is wrong**, because:
- IRS mileage is a tax benchmark, not a required employer reimbursement level,
- your cleaners also receive base job pay,
- some travel burden is already absorbed by flat job economics.

But it **does** mean long-drive jobs are the first place cleaner dissatisfaction may appear if actual drive time is high.

## 4. Profitability / affordability / cleaner-pay balance
### Where your model looks strong
1. **Base tiers are commercially sane**
   - Small and mid-sized properties do not look absurdly cheap or absurdly expensive.
2. **Add-ons still leave retained margin**
   - After locking payout shares, same-day, deep clean, linen, pet, and trash still retain platform dollars.
3. **Cleaner shares are meaningful**
   - 60–67% shares on labor-heavy add-ons are likely easy to explain and attractive psychologically.

### Where the model is vulnerable
1. **Mileage under-compensation risk on outer-zone jobs**
   - especially Creede / tertiary travel days.
2. **QA failure / re-clean scenarios**
   - stress model already showed deep-clean + re-clean admin can compress margin toward ~11%.
3. **Failed access / late checkout friction**
   - these events consume margin quickly unless surcharge collection is real, consistent, and enforceable.
4. **Cleaner effective hourly pay uncertainty**
   - flat payouts look fine, but the real question is what a cleaner nets per total labor hour including travel.

## 5. Sweet-spot assessment
### Current position
Based on the benchmarks available, your current model appears to sit in a **reasonable provisional sweet spot** for launch:
- **host affordability:** acceptable
- **cleaner pay attractiveness:** probably acceptable in core zones
- **platform retained margin:** viable on clean-path jobs and still positive on many stressed jobs

### But the sweet spot is conditional
It is only a real sweet spot if these conditions hold:
- Tier 1–4 jobs stay within the planned time windows,
- same-day approval remains constrained,
- outer-zone jobs do not dominate the mix,
- surcharge enforcement is real,
- re-clean frequency stays low,
- cleaner travel dissatisfaction does not emerge at the current mileage rate.

## 6. My recommendation
### Keep for now
- Base host price ladder
- Base cleaner pay ladder
- Add-on pricing
- Add-on payout shares

### Watch closely in pilots
- Cleaner complaints about long-drive jobs
- Acceptance rates for tertiary-zone jobs
- Effective hourly cleaner earnings on same-day and deep-clean jobs
- Re-clean rate by tier
- Late-checkout and failed-access frequency

### Most likely future adjustments
If the pilot shows pressure, the first places to tune are:
1. **outer-zone mileage or remote-zone surcharge**
2. **same-day surcharge for high-complexity jobs**
3. **Creede / tertiary-zone service restrictions**
4. **restricted-host friction surcharge**

## 7. Bottom line
Your current pricing is **not obviously underpriced** against available market references, and it is **not obviously exploitative** on cleaner pay for core-zone work.

The likely weak point is **rural travel economics**, not the base tier ladder itself.

That means the probable launch sweet spot is:
- keep core-zone pricing as-is,
- keep payout shares as-is,
- be conservative on outer-zone same-day,
- be ready to increase remote-zone or long-drive economics if cleaner behavior shows strain.
